# Getting started with Feedback

Feedback is a place to share unfinished music and get comments pinned to exact moments in a track. Everything you upload is **private until you change it**.

## 1. Get in

Sign up at [feedback.community](https://feedback.community). For now, accounts are invite only: until someone on the team approves yours, you'll see a "you're on the list" screen. You can still listen to public music while you wait.

Once you're in, open **Profile** to set a display name (it's what people see on your comments). If you also pick a handle, you get a public page at `/@yourhandle`.

## 2. Upload a rough idea

Drop a file on **Tracks**, or choose one. Before anything uploads, a dialog asks for the title, an optional project, a genre, and what you want feedback on.

- Formats: MP3, WAV, M4A, AAC, FLAC, Ogg
- Up to 200 MB and 20 minutes per file
- Tags and other metadata in your file are stripped before anyone hears it

## 3. Keep versions

When the song changes, upload a **new version** on the track page instead of a new track. Each version keeps its own comments, and you can A/B any two versions in the player to compare them. Group tracks into a **project** (single, EP, album, compilation) to play them in order.

## 4. Choose who can hear it

Open **Sharing** on a track or project. There are three choices:

| Setting                | Who can listen                                                                      |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Only you** (default) | Just you. Links do nothing.                                                         |
| **Anyone with a link** | People you send a link to, and anyone they forward it to. It isn't listed anywhere. |
| **Anyone**             | Everyone on the internet, signed in or not.                                         |

> [!WARNING]
> **Only choose "Anyone" if you're fine with anyone at all hearing it.**
> A public track shows up on your profile, on the home page, in search, and in your followers' feeds. Strangers can play it without an account.
> If you only want a few people to hear something, use **Anyone with a link** instead, and send the link only to them.

A few more things worth knowing:

- **Links can be forwarded.** Anyone holding a link can listen, so treat it like a key. Give it an expiry (1, 7, or 30 days) if you can, and turn it off when you're done. Turning a link off works straight away.
- **Projects pass their setting to their tracks.** A track in a project follows the project's setting unless you set it more strictly. Moving a track into a project never makes it more public than it was.
- **Making something private takes a few minutes to fully land.** New plays stop at once, but someone already listening can keep playing for up to 10 minutes.
- **Nobody else can download your files.** Listeners can stream, never download your originals.

## 5. Get feedback

Write a short "what I want feedback on" note on each version so listeners know what to listen for. Signed-in listeners can then comment at a moment on the waveform or generally, and reply to each other. You'll get a notification (the bell) for each new comment. Mark threads **resolved** as you work through them, then upload the next version.

Comments need an account: people listening signed out can hear the track and read your prompt, but not comments.
